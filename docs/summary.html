Monitoring, Corrective Actions & Summary

4. Procedures for Monitoring and Reporting Results

4.a. A description of the water quality monitoring program to be implemented

2027 RA Update: No change. Existing monitoring includes monthly ambient collections by the Environmental Protection Commission of Hillsborough County, Manatee County, and Pinellas County. Samples from over 100 stations baywide are collected and analyzed monthly. These regional programs are considered among the top in the nation. Additional research investments to understand Old Tampa Bay’s Pyrodinium bahamense bloom dynamics and internal nutrient fluxes have been made or are underway through Tampa Bay Environmental Restoration Fund (TBERF) grants.

4.b. Quality Assurance/Quality Control elements of monitoring

2027 RA Update: No change. All county monitoring programs and laboratories have State-approved Quality Assurance Plans and comply with FDEP’s QA rule (Chapter 62-160), including FDEP-approved SOPs. All participating county laboratories are NELAC certified. Quarterly round-robin inter-laboratory comparisons are conducted by the Southwest Florida Regional Ambient Monitoring Program (SWFL RAMP).

4.c. Procedures for entering all appropriate data into WIN/STORET:

2027 RA Update: No change. County programs comply with the latest WIN data-upload procedures developed by FDEP. Through the SWFL RAMP, all Tampa Bay water quality monitoring data providers upload data to WIN.

4.d. Responsible monitoring and reporting entity:

2027 RA Update: No change. The three entities identified in Section 4.a continue to collect water quality data. TBEP staff and the NMC’s technical support contractor assimilate and summarize the data annually to provide compliance assessment reports to FDEP. Reports for the 2022–2026 RA period are at https://tbep.org/our-work/boards-committees/nitrogen-management-consortium/.

4.e. Frequency and reporting format for reporting monitoring results:

2027 RA Update: No change. As part of this update, the TBEP and TBNMC funded development of estimated TN, TP, TSS, and BOD loads for the watershed over the 2022–2026 RA period (https://tbep.org/tampa-bay-nitrogen-loads/). The Consortium also updated a Screening Level Tool for Estimating Annual Hydrologic Loadings to Tampa Bay to assess annual compliance of hydrologically-adjusted allocations; these adjustment factors are reported annually within the NMC compliance assessment reports submitted to FDEP and EPA.

4.f. Frequency and format for reporting on the implementation of all proposed management activities:

2027 RA Update: No change. Formal reporting of results and action-plan projects continues every 5 years. Annual assessment of bay water quality conditions continues during the 2027–2031 RA Implementation Period.

4.g. Methods for evaluating progress towards goals:

2027 RA Update: No change. Formal reporting of chlorophyll-a threshold compliance continues annually and integrates new seagrass acreage estimates or trends. If exceedances occur in two consecutive years in any bay segment, additional assessment steps are required. Additionally, the TBNMC re-assessed the assimilative capacity of the Old Tampa Bay segment over the 2022–2026 RA implementation period; revisions to bay-segment load targets, entity allocations, and numeric nutrient criteria were not recommended by the NMC, with FDEP concurring on Mar. 31, 2026 that additional projects and research be pursued in Old Tampa Bay to maintain its reasonable assurance determination.

5. Description of Proposed Corrective Actions

2027 RA Update: No annual exceedances of the chlorophyll-a threshold occurred in any bay segment during the 2022–2026 RA period. Bay managers continue to pursue management actions to improve Old Tampa Bay’s ecological condition and water quality. Cooperative projects implemented over the period — many supported by the Tampa Bay Environmental Restoration Fund (TBERF) — include, among others:

  • Characterizing Seagrass Stressors in Old Tampa Bay (UF; 2026 TBERF) — stable-isotope and benthic-flux investigation of sulfide, light, and nutrient stress near seagrass transects.
  • Oyster Reef Installations (Oyster River Ecology; 2026 TBERF) — ~44,000 ft² of oyster reef habitat, including Safety Harbor (OTB) and Manatee River sites.
  • Cypress Point Living Shoreline (Tampa Bay Watch; 2025 TBERF) — oyster reefs and coastal wetland plantings in Old Tampa Bay.
  • Micronutrient Drivers of Pyrodinium Blooms (USF; 2025 TBERF) — role of vitamin B12 and bacterial communities in OTB harmful algal blooms.
  • City of St. Petersburg Septic-to-Sewer Conversions (2024 TBERF) — ~90 lbs/yr TN precluded; completed Nov. 2025.
  • Old Tampa Bay Green Infrastructure Tool (FSU; 2022 TBERF) — web decision-support tool for the Sweetwater Creek watershed; completed Mar. 2026.

Additional habitat-restoration, stormwater-infrastructure, and point-source projects across the watershed cumulatively contribute to the bay’s ecological improvement.

6. Summary of Progress Since the 2022 Reasonable Assurance Update Report

Continuing nitrogen-load-reduction efforts by NMC partners, combined with drought conditions through most of the period, supported adequate water quality for seagrass recovery baywide over 2022–2026.

Management actions & restoration investments

  • More than 525 nitrogen load-reduction projects implemented by Consortium participants since 1992; cumulatively, ~983.4 tons/yr of TN precluded from entering Tampa Bay over 1992–2026.
  • Since 1992, over $1.0 billion invested in nitrogen load-reduction projects; over $2.84 billion including land acquisition, preservation, and education (per the Tampa Bay Action Plan Database).
  • Baywide TN load reductions over 2022–2026 estimated at 33.1 tons/yr; ongoing, planned, and budgeted post-2026 projects expected to reduce TN loading by ~106.8 tons/yr at a cost of $137,428,003.
  • Consortium partners maintain TN load allocations within the federally-recognized TMDLs (Tables 10–14), with entity-generated reductions retained as indefinite credits (2009 RA Addendum §IX.D).

Environmental indicators

  • Baywide seagrass coverage in 2026 (31,563 acres) remained below the 41,655-acre 2016 peak; Old Tampa Bay continued to drive baywide loss while other segments were stable or increasing.
  • Chlorophyll-a thresholds were met across the major bay segments over 2022–2026, including the Old Tampa Bay turnaround relative to 2017–2021.
