Monitoring, Corrective Actions & Summary

4. Procedures for Monitoring and Reporting Results

4.a. Description of the water quality monitoring program to be implemented

2027 RA Update: No change. Existing monitoring includes monthly ambient collections by the Environmental Protection Commission of Hillsborough County, Manatee County, and Pinellas County. Samples from over 100 stations baywide are collected and analyzed monthly. These regional programs are considered among the top in the nation.1 Additional research investments to understand Old Tampa Bay’s Pyrodinium bahamense bloom dynamics and internal nutrient fluxes have been made or are underway through TBEP and partner funded projects and/or though Tampa Bay Environmental Restoration Fund (TBERF) grants.

4.b. Quality Assurance/Quality Control elements of monitoring

2027 RA Update: No change. All county monitoring programs and laboratories have State-approved Quality Assurance Plans and comply with FDEP’s QA rule (Chapter 62-160), including FDEP-approved SOPs. All participating county laboratories are NELAC certified. Quarterly round-robin inter-laboratory comparisons are conducted by the Southwest Florida Regional Ambient Monitoring Program (SWFL RAMP).

4.c. Procedures for entering all appropriate data into WIN/STORET:

2027 RA Update: No change. County programs comply with the latest WIN data-upload procedures developed by FDEP. Through the SWFL RAMP, all Tampa Bay water quality monitoring data providers upload data to WIN.

4.d. Responsible monitoring and reporting entity:

2027 RA Update: No change. The three entities identified in Section 4.a continue to collect water quality data. TBEP staff and the NMC’s technical support contractor assimilate and summarize the data annually to provide compliance assessment reports to FDEP. Reports for the 2022–2026 RA period are at https://tbep.org/our-work/boards-committees/nitrogen-management-consortium/ (see NMC Publications sidebar menu).

4.e. Frequency and reporting format for reporting monitoring results:

2027 RA Update: No change. As part of this update, the TBEP and TBNMC funded the development of estimated TN, TP, TSS, and BOD loads for the watershed over the 2022–2026 RA period (https://tbep.org/tampa-bay-nitrogen-loads/). The Consortium also updated a Screening Level Tool for Estimating Annual Hydrologic Loadings to Tampa Bay to assess annual compliance of hydrologically-adjusted allocations. These adjustment factors are reported annually within the NMC compliance assessment reports submitted to FDEP and EPA.

4.f. Frequency and format for reporting on the implementation of all proposed management activities:

2027 RA Update: No change. Formal reporting of results and action-plan projects continues every 5 years. Annual assessment of bay water quality conditions continues during the 2027–2031 RA Implementation Period.

4.g. Methods for evaluating progress towards goals:

2027 RA Update: No change. Formal reporting of chlorophyll-a threshold compliance continues annually and integrates new seagrass acreage estimates or trends. If exceedances occur in two consecutive years in any bay segment, additional assessment steps are required. Additionally, the TBNMC re-assessed the assimilative capacity of the Old Tampa Bay segment over the 2022–2026 RA implementation period.2, 3, 4 Revisions to bay-segment load targets, entity allocations, and numeric nutrient criteria were not recommended by the NMC (see Decision Memo 2027-03), with FDEP concurring on Mar. 31, 2026 that additional projects and research should be pursued in Old Tampa Bay to maintain its reasonable assurance determination.

5. Description of Proposed Corrective Actions

2027 RA Update: No annual exceedances of the chlorophyll-a threshold occurred in any bay segment during the 2022–2026 RA period. Bay managers continue to pursue management actions to improve Old Tampa Bay’s ecological condition and water quality. Cooperative projects implemented over the period — many supported by the Tampa Bay Environmental Restoration Fund (TBERF) — include, among others:

  • Characterizing Seagrass Stressors in Old Tampa Bay (Research; 2026 TBERF): The University of Florida will characterize seagrass stressors in Old Tampa Bay to support ongoing water quality and seagrass management activities. Investigating stressors that may contribute to seagrass loss will be carried out by: (1) determining if there are indications of seagrass stress from sulfide toxicity, light, or nutrient status by measuring elemental stoichiometry and multiple stable isotopes near existing seagrass monitoring transects, and (2) deploying benthic flux chambers to quantify sediment sulfide and nutrient fluxes within Old Tampa Bay.
  • Oyster Reef Installations (Habitat Restoration; 2026 TBERF): Oyster River Ecology, LLC (ORE) is creating 44,000 square feet of oyster reef habitat in Tampa Bay and obtaining permits for approximately five acres within Old Tampa Bay for future oyster restoration. The project includes three work areas (two sites in Safety Harbor of Old Tampa Bay and one site in the Manatee River). Approximately, 4,000 Oyster Reef Pots (ORPs) will be constructed and installed at the Pinellas County Philippe Park Living Shoreline Project’s 0.64-acre reef (ERP #43002320.001); 20,000 ORPs will be constructed and installed at the 5-acre ORE’s Safety Harbor site; and 20,000 ORPs will be constructed and installed at Eileen Reef in the Manatee River (ORE Management Agreement 41-MA-2231).
  • Cypress Point Living Shoreline (Habitat Restoration; 2025 TBERF): Tampa Bay Watch is constructing a living shoreline project that includes the creation of oyster reefs and the installation of coastal wetland plants along the shoreline of Cypress Point Park in Old Tampa Bay. The project is in conceptual form and with TBERF grant support will be fully designed, permitted and implementation will begin. Project implementation will include the installation of 450 reef balls, 82 tons of recycled and fossilized oyster shells and 3 marsh grass plantings with community volunteers.
  • Micronutrient Drivers of Pyrodinium Blooms (Research; 2025 TBERF): The University of South Florida is investigating the role of vitamin B12 and bacterial communities in driving Pyrodinium bahamense harmful algal blooms (HABs) in Old Tampa Bay (OTB). Despite nitrogen reduction efforts, OTB continues to experience severe HABs, prompting the need to explore micronutrient drivers. Preliminary research indicates P. bahamense has a high B12 requirement, with potential sources including wastewater, stormwater, and mangrove-associated bacteria. Leveraging advanced mass spectrometry and microbial community analysis, this study will quantify B12 levels, identify B12 sources to Old Tampa Bay, and determine if B12 availability promotes P. bahamense bloom formation.
  • City of St. Petersburg Septic-to-Sewer Conversions (Stormwater Restoration; 2024 TBERF): This project supported sewer conversions for the City’s remaining septic systems. The City currently has around 17 remaining known septic systems, 6 are scattered throughout the city, while 11 are concentrated in the Master’s Bayou area near Weedon Island Preserve and Old Tampa Bay. The City utilized CIP funding to extend the sewer system to these 11 properties, and the TBERF project funded a pilot program to conduct outreach and financially assist 11 homeowners with septic-to-sewer conversions. Approximately, 90 lbs./yr of TN was precluded from entering Old and Middle Tampa Bays. The City completed the project in Nov. 2025.
  • Philippe Park Living Shoreline Phase 1 & 2 (Education, Stormwater & Habitat Restoration; 2019 & 2023 TBERF): This Pinellas County living shoreline project enhanced 2,600 linear feet of Old Tampa Bay shoreline and improved water quality by reducing sedimentation, establishing filter-feeding oysters, and installing native vegetation. Oyster spat monitoring continued through Dec. 2026.
  • Old Tampa Bay Green Infrastructure Tool (Stormwater & Habitat Restoration; 2022 TBERF): Florida State University developed an interactive, web-based decision support tool for planners and decision makers to evaluate the impact of sea level rise and climate change on nutrient pollution in the Sweetwater Creek watershed in Old Tampa Bay and identify the most resilient and cost-effective green infrastructure to mitigate the impacts of nutrients in the face of climate change and sea level rise. This project was completed in Mar. 2026.
  • Controlling Pyrodinium Outbreaks with Low-Cost Biochar (Research; 2022 TBERF): This Florida Institute of Technology project assessed the ability of low-cost biochars to control outbreaks of Pyrodinium bahamense in Old Tampa Bay. A diverse team from the Florida Institute of Technology (FIT) and Mote Marine Laboratory explored whether commercial biochars produced from waste biomass can be used to control algae growth and saxitoxin production. A final report was completed in Apr. 2026.
  • Caulerpa-Seagrass Interactions in Tampa Bay (Research; 2022 TBERF): This University of Tampa project evaluated the impacts of changes from seagrass meadows to Caulerpa spp. by conducting epifaunal sampling and field experiments to determine whether the habitats provide comparable ecosystem services. The project aimed to better understand Caulerpa spp. colonization rates in the bay. The project was completed in Apr. 2026 and a manuscript is in prep.
  • Up in the Air, Down in the Water: Atmospheric Deposition in Tampa Bay (Research & Education; 2021 TBERF): This University of Florida research and IFAS extension/education project aimed to better understand the contribution of atmospheric deposition to N loads in stormwater and the potential impact of atmospherically derived N on water quality in the Tampa Bay watershed. The project produced data on the forms and concentrations of N in wet atmospheric deposition before and after it interacts with the urban tree canopy and urban impervious surfaces and characterized the degree to which atmospheric N is utilized by Karenia brevis and Pyrodinium bahamense, the toxic microalgae most responsible for algal problems in Old Tampa Bay. A peer-reviewed publication was developed (https://doi.org/10.1016/j.wroa.2025.100431), and the project was completed in Apr. 2026.
  • Channel 5 / Roosevelt Creek Basin Restoration (Stormwater & Habitat Restoration; SEP Funding): The TBEP was identiﬁed as a recipient of $106,800 to be directed towards a public interest project focused on improving the environmental quality of Old Tampa Bay and/or its watershed in the vicinity of the City of Largo. Funds were received in Jun. 2021 and Aug. 2023, and a partnership with Pinellas County was identified to remove a non-functioning weir and conduct habitat restoration associated with Channel 5 / Roosevelt Creek which drains into Old Tampa Bay in the vicinity of Feather Sound. Project components included construction activities around the modified weir, bank stabilization and grading, exotic plant and debris removal, and native plant installation. Pinellas County completed the project in Jun. 2026.
  • Tampa Bay Regional Stormwater Controls Identification & Implementation (Research, Stormwater & Habitat Restoration; FDEP RESTORE FPL 3b Grant): The TBEP, in partnership with the Tampa Bay Regional Planning Council, submitted a grant application to FDEP’s RESTORE Funded Priorities List 3b solicitation in July 2022 and the grant was awarded in Sep. 2024. This two-phase project seeks to develop a multi-jurisdictional stormwater master plan in the Old Tampa Bay watershed and implement high priority stormwater retrofits to reduce nonpoint source nutrient loads in the region. A surface water resource assessment and preliminary project area inventory were developed. Next steps involve the OTB Working Group of the NMC to prioritize and advance 1-3 project areas toward full design and permits. This is anticipated to occur over the 2027-2028 period.
  • Cooper’s Point Tidal Circulation Master Plan (Stormwater & Habitat Restoration; 2026 NEP IIJA Funding): The City of Clearwater is currently developing a master plan for the 645 acres surrounding Cooper’s Bayou in OTB. Cooper’s Bayou has poor tidal circulation, mucky sediments, and sporadic seagrass coverage. As part of the master plan development, the City is exploring tidal circulation, habitat restoration, stormwater retrofits and community recreational enhancements throughout the 380-acre Cooper’s Point Preserve portion of the project area. The City is currently soliciting Contractor support to more fully develop the design and permitting for several components of the master plan. TBEP’s priorities in this portion of Old Tampa Bay have been to improve overall water quality, reduce the propensity of harmful algal blooms (Pyrodinium bahamense), and restore lost seagrass coverage through construction of additional causeway breaches.

Additional habitat-restoration, stormwater-infrastructure, and point-source projects across the watershed cumulatively contribute to the bay’s ecological improvement.

6. Summary of Progress Since the 2022 Reasonable Assurance Update Report

Continuing nitrogen-load-reduction efforts by NMC partners, combined with drought conditions through most of the period, supported adequate water quality for seagrass recovery baywide over 2022–2026. At the beginning of 2026, baywide seagrass coverage was estimated to be XX,XXX acres. A summary of progress since the 2022 Tampa Bay Reasonable Assurance Plan Update submittal is below.

Management actions & restoration investments{.underline}

  • More than 525 nitrogen load-reduction projects implemented by Consortium participants since 1992. Cumulatively, ~983.4 tons/yr of TN precluded from entering Tampa Bay from 1992–2026.
  • Since 1992, over $1.0 billion directly invested in nitrogen load-reduction projects. Over $2.84 billion has been invested when considering other land acquisition, preservation, and education projects that have potential nitrogen load reduction benefits to downstream waters of the estuary (per the Tampa Bay Action Plan Database).
  • Baywide TN load reductions from 2022–2026 are estimated at 33.1 tons/yr. Ongoing, planned, and budgeted projects post-2026 are expected to reduce TN loading by ~106.8 tons/yr at a cost of $137,428,003.
  • Consortium partners have agreed to maintain TN load allocations within the federally-recognized TMDLs (Tables 10–14), with entity-generated reductions retained as credits to each entity (2009 RA Addendum §IX.D).

Environmental indicators{.underline}

  • Baywide seagrass coverage in 2026 (31,563 acres) remained below the 41,655-acre 2016 peak. Old Tampa Bay continued to see losses while other bay segments showed increases.
  • Chlorophyll-a thresholds were met across all the major bay segments from 2022–2026, including in Old Tampa Bay. This is a significant improvement relative to the 2017–2021 RA period in Old Tampa Bay.
