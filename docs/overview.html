Reaonable Assurance Plan Update Overview

1. Description of the Waterbody

The Tampa Bay estuary is located on the eastern shore of the Gulf of Mexico in Florida (Figure 1). At 882 km², it is Florida’s largest open-water estuary. More than 3 million people live in the 5,870 km² watershed. Land use in the watershed is mixed — about 32% undeveloped, 17% agricultural, 42% urban and suburban residential, and the remaining 9% mining. Major habitats include mangroves, salt marshes, salt barrens, and submerged aquatic vegetation.

Between 1950 and 1988, an estimated 42% of the seagrass acreage in Tampa Bay was lost, primarily through excess nitrogen loading and related increases in phytoplankton concentrations that limited light for seagrass survival and growth. In 1980, all municipal wastewater treatment plants were required to provide 100% reuse or Advanced Wastewater Treatment (AWT) for discharges directly to the bay and its tributaries. Together with stormwater regulations enacted in the 1980s, significant nitrogen loads reductions to the bay occurred. In hindsight, 1976 TN loadings were more than three times greater than current estimates (1976 = 9,904 tons TN/yr vs. a 2020s annual average of 3,321 tons TN/yr; see: https://tbep.org/tampa-bay-nitrogen-loads/).

The Tampa Bay Nitrogen Management Consortium (NMC) was formed in 1996 and includes local governments and agencies, phosphate companies, port industries, electric utilities, and agricultural interests that work cooperatively in a voluntary framework to maintain nitrogen loads supporting seagrass restoration. In 2020, the TBEP and partners updated the Tampa Bay Habitat Master Plan and adopted the goal of maintaining at least 40,000 acres of seagrass baywide.

Figure 1: R/figs.R → current land use figure · generated from repository code.

Recent data and observations from Tampa Bay indicate that continuing efforts to reduce nitrogen loading by the NMC partners are resulting in water quality sufficient to maintain stable seagrasses in most bay segments. Time series plots show that FDEP-adopted chlorophyll-a thresholds have been met in all major bay segments over the 2022-2026 RA period (Figure 2). These results represent improvement in overall water quality conditions in Tampa Bay, particularly in the Old Tampa Bay (OTB) segment which did not meet chlorophyll-a numeric nutrient criteria in four of the five years represented in the 2022 RA Update period (2017 - 2021).

Figure 2: reproduced from the 2027 RA Update; equivalently generated by R/figs.R → annual chl means all segments (show_rathrplot(), raanlz_avedat()), from data/chldat.RData.

Consequently between 2022 and 2026, seagrass coverage increased by 1,426 acres baywide. However, the Old Tampa Bay segment continued to see losses. Baywide seagrass coverage was estimated at 31,563 acres in 2024 (Figure 3), remaining below the 40,000-acre protection and recovery goal for a second consecutive RA assessment period. Historic changes by segment (Figure 4) show the long-term increase to the 2016 baywide maximum; coverage increased in all segments during 2022–2026 except Old Tampa Bay.

1.a. Name:

2027 RA Update: No change. This document addresses the four main bay segments of Tampa Bay: Old Tampa Bay, Hillsborough Bay, Middle Tampa Bay and Lower Tampa Bay. As of the 2009 RA Addendum, an additional segment was included: “Remainder of Lower Tampa Bay,” which includes Terra Ceia Bay and portions of the Manatee River and southern Boca Ciega Bay (Figure 5). The Old Tampa Bay, Hillsborough Bay, Middle Tampa Bay and Lower Tampa Bay segments are identical to FDEP’s established estuary nutrient regions; the Remainder of Lower Tampa Bay RA Segment includes FDEP’s Boca Ciega Bay South, Terra Ceia Bay, and Manatee River Estuary nutrient regions (62-302.532(1), F.A.C.).

1.g. Area of the waterbody:

2027 RA Update: No change. The total surface area of the four major bay segments in Tampa Bay is 882 km² (approximately 341 square miles). When including the Remainder of Lower Tampa Bay, the total surface area is 1,006 km² (approximately 389 square miles).

1.h. Pollutant(s) of concern:

2027 RA Update: No change. The pollutant of concern is Total Nitrogen (TN), the limiting nutrient in Tampa Bay. Elevated nitrogen loading leads to excess algal growth (indicated by chlorophyll-a concentrations), which reduces light penetration and causes seagrass coverage loss.

1.i. Suspected or documented sources of pollutant of concern:

2027 RA Update: The TBEP has developed loading reports for Tampa Bay since 1985. The most recent estimates cover the 2022–2026 period and are openly accessible at https://tbep.org/tampa-bay-nitrogen-loads. Table 1 shows the proportion of major contributing sources of TN load to Tampa Bay over the current and prior, 5-year RA assessment periods. Total nitrogen loadings are less variable than during the 1985 to early-2000s period (Figure 7). In 2024, loadings were significantly influenced by excessive rainfall stemming from three tropical-storm systems. 2. Description of Water Quality or Aquatic Ecological Goals

2.a. Water quality-based targets or aquatic ecological goals:

2027 RA Update: No change. The baywide seagrass restoration goal of 40,000 acres was adopted in 2020. Maintaining adequate water quality at adopted FDEP chlorophyll-a threshold levels in each major bay segment on an annual-average basis is the primary metric for assessing progress toward the seagrass restoration goal (see Figure 2). A secondary goal — maintaining hydrologically-normalized total nitrogen loads to each major bay segment — was adopted with the 2009 RA Addendum and later codified as the numeric nutrient criteria for the Tampa Bay estuarine nutrient regions (62-302.532(1), F.A.C.). A compliance assessment framework was established and has been utilized for all subsequent reasonable assurance updates (see 2009 RA Addendum, Section VIII).

2.b. Averaging period:

2027 RA Update: No change. The TBEP uses annual-average bay-segment uncorrected chlorophyll-a levels for tracking attainment, as approved by FDEP under the original 2002 RA submittal and adopted in 2012 as NNC under subsection 62-302.532(1), F.A.C. for Old Tampa Bay, Hillsborough Bay, Middle Tampa Bay and Lower Tampa Bay (the same value is used for assessing the Remainder of LTB). An annual evaluation of TN load attainment is predicated on the water quality assessment as outlined in the compliance assessment framework identified above. Annual bay segment assessment methods are documented in the tbeptools introduction; source code is at tbep-tech/wq-dash.

2.c. How will goals result in restoration of impaired designated uses:

2027 RA Update: No change. Maintaining chlorophyll-a concentrations at target levels is expected to maintain water clarity adequate to support seagrass at or above 40,000 acres throughout Tampa Bay, ensuring that external nutrient loads do not result in an imbalance in the flora or fauna of Tampa Bay.

2.d. Determining whether additional corrective actions are needed:

2027 RA Update: No changes to the TBEP’s annual management Decision Matrix process, the Tampa Bay NMC nutrient management strategy, or the allocation compliance assessment framework. Results of the bay-segment-specific compliance assessment over the 2022–2026 RA period are shown in Figure 11 and described in Tables 2–6. For the 2022–2026 RA period, no annual exceedances of the chlorophyll-a thresholds nor of hydrologically-normalized total nitrogen loads were observed. Likewise, TN and TP numeric nutrient criteria (Figure 9) were not exceeded. (2026 outcomes are shown as pending until the period closes.)

3.b. Existing and proposed management activities:

2027 RA Update: Consortium participants have documented completed and planned TN load-reduction projects in the online Tampa Bay Action Plan Database (https://apdb.tbep.org). Completed, ongoing, and future projects for the period are listed in Tables 7–9 (see the Action Plan & Allocation Tables chapter). Corrective-action and restoration projects are summarized in the Summary chapter (Section 5).

Over the 2022-2026 RA implementation period, 29 projects were implemented in the Tampa Bay watershed by Consortium participants. Cumulatively, for those projects that have load reduction estimates associated with them, a TN load reduction of 33.1 tons/year was estimated over the 2022-2026 period (Table 7). This amount would likely be larger if estimates were developed for all projects.

In addition, Consortium participants continue to look towards the future and have plans to implement or continue to implement a number of projects post-2026. Table 8 lists projects that are being continued by Consortium participants and include education components. Combining information from both tables’ projects where load reductions can be estimated, approximately 106.8 tons/year of TN is estimated to be precluded from entering the bay in the future.

3.f. How will future growth and new sources be addressed:

2027 RA Update: Consistent with prior RA approvals and the 2009 RA Addendum, allocations to all TN loading sources are formalized and cumulatively capped at the federally-recognized TMDL for each bay segment. Reviews of interim, new, and transferred sources led to formal NMC concurrence of allocations assigned to each entity for the 2027–2031 RA implementation period (Tables 10–14, in the Action Plan & Allocation Tables chapter), remaining within the TMDL limits.

Lastly, the Tampa Bay NMC has developed recommended protocols to address any unforeseen need for a TN load allocation. Consortium participants have previously formalized a process to address new, missed or expanded TN sources in future RA updates, and FDEP has concurred with those recommendations. Lastly, during the development of the 2017 RA Update, Consortium participants developed recommendations for assignment of any unassigned reservation allocation that may occur in the future (see Appendix 2 of the 2017 RA Update).

3.g. Confirmed sources of funding:

2027 RA Update: No change. Consortium participants abide by recommended funding levels for the NMC’s process to deliver 5-year RA updates to the FDEP. For each 5-year RA period, all participating NMC entities with a 5-year annual average allocation less than one ton TN per year are requested to contribute a recommended nominal amount of $750, and all other sources are requested to contribute a one-time recommended amount of $7,500. Consortium contributions are due generally in Years 1-2 of each RA cycle, to allow time for development of the technical basis required for an update. All entities with allocations or providing regulatory oversight/input to the RA Update process (in-kind support) are invited to attend and encouraged to participate in all Consortium meetings and deliberations in the future. A chronology of NMC activities and meetings can be found at https://tbep.org/our-work/boards-committees/nitrogen-management-consortium/.

In addition, Consortium participants have planned investments of $137,428,003 to continue projects listed in Table 8 and implement new projects listed in Table 9.

3.h. Implementation schedule:

2027 RA Update: No change. Consortium participants continue to develop future load reduction projects and document them in the online Tampa Bay Action Plan Database (https://apdb.tbep.org). Table 8 lists ongoing projects. Table 9 lists projects planned to be initiated/completed after 2026.

3.i. Enforcement programs, if the management strategy is not voluntary:

2027 RA Update: No change. Participation in the Consortium remains voluntary, and NMC partners continue to encourage non-participating point and nonpoint sources to join. The allocation compliance assessment process (Section 2.a) continues during the 2027–2031 RA implementation period; an assessment of the 2022–2026 period is in Appendices 1–5. FDEP and other regulatory agencies continue to ensure permitted facilities meet all permit requirements through existing regulatory and enforcement programs.
